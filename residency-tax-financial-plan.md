## 0. TL;DR — the decision in one page

**Your profile:** Ukrainian citizen, under 26, mid-level Java/Spring backend engineer,
likely on Temporary Protection (status UKR) in Poland. Weighing an Aristocrat UoP offer
(~18–25k gross) vs a Zoolatech B2B offer, plus a ~14k B2B side gig, planning a Warsaw

**Core recommendations:**

2. **Passport goal → decide by where you'll actually live.** Polish and German passports

3. **Income → Aristocrat UoP (main) + B2B side**, using the under-26 PIT-0 window and
   ryczałt 12% on B2B. This is legal on **both** CUKR and Blue Card.

4. **PPK → stay in.** The employer match is free money, the account is yours even if you
   leave Poland, and (under 26 + first-home withdrawal) it's especially efficient for you.

5. **Timing pressure:** the under-26 tax window, the CUKR application window, and pending

## 1. Residence status — CUKR vs EU Blue Card

### 1.1 What each one is

| | **CUKR card** | **EU Blue Card** |
|---|---|---|
| Who it's for | Ukrainians who held UKR status (Temporary Protection) | Any non-EU highly-qualified worker |
| Nature | Special 3-year temporary residence permit | Highly-qualified-work temporary residence permit |
| Validity | 3 years | Contract + 3 months, max 3 years |
| Cost | ~340 PLN + card fee | Higher, employer paperwork |
| Salary floor | **None** | **13,355.34 PLN gross/mo (2026)** = 150% avg salary |
| Contract type | Any (UoP, B2B, mix) | **UoP only** for the qualifying job |
| Application | Online via MOS | Voivode, with employer |

**CUKR eligibility:** held UKR status on the **4 June 2025** cut-off, still hold it, and
have **365 days continuous** UKR status. Application window runs into 2027.
**Trade-off:** issuing CUKR **ends your UKR status** → you lose UKR-tied perks (free NFZ
healthcare, free university tuition). If employed / paying contributions, healthcare is
covered anyway, so for a working person this is minor. Revoked if you leave Poland >6 months.

### 1.2 B2B side hustle — allowed on BOTH (since 1 June 2025)

- **CUKR:** B2B / JDG allowed with **no limits** — it can be your entire income.
- **Blue Card:** B2B / JDG allowed **as a supplement only** — your qualifying UoP (at the
  salary floor) must remain the *main* basis; B2B must stay secondary. Register the JDG in
  **CEIDG** with your residence card annotated *"Niebieska Karta UE"*.

So your **"Aristocrat UoP + B2B side"** structure is legal on either. The difference:
- On **Blue Card**, B2B must stay a genuine *side* thing.
- On **CUKR**, you can scale B2B up to whatever you want, or go pure B2B.

### 1.3 The one thing only the Blue Card gives you

| | CUKR | Blue Card |
|---|---|---|
| B2B flexibility | ✅ Unlimited | ✅ Supplement only |
| Counts toward PL long-term residence | ✅ | ✅ |
| **Work in Germany / other EU** | ❌ Poland only | ✅ Relocate after 12 months |
| Aggregate residence across EU states | ❌ | ✅ |
| Absences allowed | 6 months max | 12 mo/absence, 18 mo total |
| Independent of a specific job | ✅ | ❌ (lose job → 3–6 mo to replace) |

*Schengen travel on CUKR = 90/180 tourism, **not** work.*

### 1.4 Recommendation

**Get CUKR** unless you specifically want the Germany/EU work option. It does everything
your income strategy needs, keeps B2B fully open, costs almost nothing, and counts toward
permanent residence. You can switch to a Blue Card later if Germany becomes a real goal.

## 2. Does Temporary Protection time count toward permanent residence? ⚖️ **UNSETTLED**

This is the single most important open question for your timeline, and it is **genuinely
not settled.**

### 2.1 What IS settled
- **You cannot apply for the long-term resident permit while on Temporary Protection**
  (Art. 213(1)(e) Aliens Act; Art. 3(2)(a) EU Directive 2003/109). You must **convert to
  CUKR** (or another temporary permit) first.
- **CUKR time counts** toward the 5-year clock. Undisputed.

### 2.2 What is DISPUTED — do the *prior* UKR years (2022→now) count once you convert?

| | **Restrictive view (official-leaning)** | **Generous view (some lawyers)** |
|---|---|---|
| Held by | Head of Office for Foreigners (Szef UDSC); several voivodeships | Association for Legal Intervention; some immigration lawyers |
| Position | UKR/TP period does **NOT** count; clock starts at CUKR | The 2022–2026 TP residence **was legal** and **counts in full** |
| Basis | Art. 3(2)(a) Directive excludes TP from scope | Stay was lawful; Art. 213 read purposively |

### 2.3 Why it's undefined
- **The EU fix is dead** (see §4) — the reform that would explicitly count TP time is
  stalled in the Council indefinitely.
- **No binding CJEU ruling** resolves it (recent TP cases address other questions).
- So it's decided by **administrative interpretation**, which currently leans **against**
  counting it.

### 2.4 Why it matters — a ~4-year swing
- **If TP counts:** ~4 yrs UKR (2022→2026) + CUKR → 5-year mark ~**2027**.
- **If it doesn't:** clock starts at CUKR (2026) → 5-year mark ~**2031**.

### 2.5 Planning rule
**Assume conservatively the clock starts at CUKR** — but position for the upside:
1. **Get CUKR soon** — every month of delay is a month that (restrictive view) doesn't count.
2. **Keep proof of continuous legal residence since 2022** (PESEL UKR, ZUS/employment, address history).
3. **When eligible, apply arguing TP counts** — with a lawyer. Little downside to asserting it.
4. **Don't build your timeline on TP years counting.** Treat it as a bonus.

## 3. Path to Permanent Residence & Citizenship — Poland vs Germany

**Both passports are EU citizenship** — identical mobility and rights. This is "which is
the fastest/easiest EU passport for you," not "which is better."

### 3.1 PLAN A — Poland passport (~8 years, fits your current life)

You **don't need the Blue Card** for this — CUKR works and keeps B2B open.

| Phase | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Durable permit | Now | CUKR (or Blue Card). Both count toward next step. |
| 2. EU long-term resident | After **5 yrs** continuous residence | Needs stable income, **B1 Polish**, health insurance. |
| 3. Citizenship (*uznanie za obywatela*) | After **3 more yrs** on that permit | **Bound decision** — meet conditions, voivode **must** grant. Needs B1 Polish + income + housing title. |
| **Total** | **~8 years** + processing | Keep Ukrainian passport (Poland tolerates dual). |

**Key thresholds (2025/2026):**
- Income for LTR: > **1,010 PLN net/mo** (single) / > **823 PLN/mo** per family member (trivial for you).
- Income assessed over **2 years** for Blue Card holders (vs 3 for others).
- **B1 Polish** required for both the LTR permit *and* citizenship — much easier for a Ukrainian than B1 German.
- Absences: LTR standard ≤6 mo single / ≤10 mo total; Blue Card holders ≤12 mo / ≤18 mo.

**Citizenship residence clock caveat:** the **3-year clock only runs while you hold the
long-term resident (or permanent-residence) permit** — CUKR/Blue Card time gets you *to*
the LTR permit but doesn't itself run the citizenship clock.

**⚠️ Risk:** Poland has **pending bills to raise the 3-year citizenship rule to 8 or 10
years** (proposed late 2025, not yet law). If passed, Plan A stretches toward ~13–15 years.
**This is the strongest argument to start the clock soon.**

**Shortcuts to check:**
- **Polish ancestry** (parent/grandparent, or 2 great-grandparents Polish) → **Karta Polaka
  → immediate permanent residence → citizenship in ~1 year.** If you have any Polish roots,
  this changes everything. **Worth checking your family tree.**
- Marriage to a Polish citizen → citizenship after 2 yrs on permanent/LTR permit + 3 yrs married.
- Presidential grant (*nadanie*) → discretionary, no minimum residence, but unpredictable and unreliable.

### 3.2 PLAN B — Germany passport (~5–6 years, but you must relocate)

**Two upfront corrections:**
- **Germany's 3-year fast-track citizenship was ABOLISHED** (repealed Oct 2025, effective
  1 Nov 2025). Standard is now **5 years + B1 German**. No sub-5-year route exists.
- **Polish Blue Card years do NOT transfer to Germany** for citizenship or the fast
  settlement permit — the clock **restarts** on arrival in Germany.

| Phase | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Get to Germany | — | **(a) Direct** German job + German Blue Card (fastest — clock starts now), or **(b) via Poland** — hold Polish Blue Card 12 months, then intra-EU mobility (easier move, but those 12 months are wasted for citizenship). |
| 2. Live/work in Germany | 5 yrs | Blue Card qualifies for naturalization directly (no settlement permit needed first). Get **B1 German**, pass the 33-question test, stay off benefits (auto-met if working full-time ≥20 of last 24 months). |
| 3. Citizenship | Apply at yr 5 | +6–18 months processing. Keep Ukrainian passport (Germany allows dual since June 2024). |
| **Total** | **~5.5–6.5 yrs** from arriving in Germany | Direct route fastest; Poland detour adds ~1 year. |

**German settlement permit (Niederlassungserlaubnis) — optional milestone, not needed for
citizenship:** fastest is **21 months** on a German Blue Card with **B1 German**, or **27
months** with A1, provided pension contributions paid throughout.

**Intra-EU mobility mechanics:** after **12 months** on a Polish Blue Card you may enter
Germany visa-free for work, but you must **apply for a German Blue Card at the local
Ausländerbehörde within 1 month of entry** (you can start working immediately while
pending). It's a *new application*, not automatic free movement.

### 3.3 Side-by-side

| | **Germany** | **Poland** |
|---|---|---|
| PR needed before citizenship? | No — Blue Card qualifies directly | Yes — LTR/PR first (~5 yrs) |
| Residence to citizenship | **5 yrs in Germany** (only track) | 5 yrs → LTR + 3 yrs on it → **~8 yrs** |
| Prior other-EU time counts? | **No** (Germany-only clock) | Partly (Blue Card EU aggregation, last 2 yrs in PL) |
| Language | B1 German | **B1 Polish** (easier for you) |
| Dual citizenship | Allowed (since 6/2024) | Tolerated (no renunciation) |
| Fast track | **None** (abolished 10/2025) | Presidential grant (unreliable); Karta Polaka ~1 yr |
| Direction of travel | Stable at 5 yrs | **Pending bills may raise 3→8/10 yrs** |

**Decisive question: are you willing to move to Germany in the next year and restart there?**
- **No / unsure →** Plan A (Poland via CUKR). Passport ~8 yrs, but fits your Warsaw life.
- **Yes, passport ASAP is the top priority →** Plan B, and go **direct** to a German Blue
  Card — don't route through Poland, since Poland time won't count.

### 3.4 Dual citizenship status
- **Poland:** tolerates it — no renunciation of Ukrainian citizenship required. On Polish
  soil you're treated solely as a Polish citizen.
- **Germany:** allows it generally since the June 2024 reform.
- **Ukraine:** multiple-citizenship law entered force **16 Jan 2026**, but only for
  countries on a Cabinet-of-Ministers list. ⚖️ Poland/Germany reported but **verify the
  decree** before relying on formal inclusion.

## 4. The EU reform that would have helped — why it's dead

The **recast of the Long-Term Residents Directive** (COM(2022) 650) would have made TP time
count and cut the residence requirement 5→3 years.

- **Parliament backed it** (ambitiously). **The Council (member states) killed it:** in
  **March 2024** a blocking minority refused the compromises, citing **sovereignty over
  immigration control**. The Council **ceased negotiations**.
- **Outlook:** no talks under the Polish (H1 2025) or Danish (H2 2025) presidencies.
  Trackers assess it **"will not be amended for the foreseeable future."** The Commission's
  current energy is on **phasing *out* temporary protection**, not rewarding it.
- **Implication:** the clean fix is off the table indefinitely. **Don't wait for it** — the
  TP-counting question will be decided by Polish administrative practice/courts, not this reform.

## 5. Income & Tax strategy

### 5.1 Under-26 PIT-0 (zero income tax)
While under 26, employment (UoP) income up to **85,528 PLN/year** is **exempt from PIT**.
This is why your net on a UoP salary is unusually high (e.g., 24k gross ≈ 17.4k net).
**This window is finite — optimize aggressively before you turn 26.**

### 5.2 UoP + B2B combined
- **Legal on both CUKR and Blue Card** (see §1.2).
- **UoP (Aristocrat):** secures mortgage capacity, PIT-0 while under 26, ZUS/health coverage,
  and (on Blue Card) satisfies the qualifying-job requirement.
- **B2B side (e.g., 14k/mo gig):** tax it under **ryczałt 12%** (IT services). With a UoP
  salary ≥ minimum wage, **ZUS social contributions on the B2B are optional** → ~84%
  retention on B2B income (12% tax + health contribution only).
- **Net effect:** the B2B side income is highly efficient stacked on top of the PIT-0 UoP.

### 5.3 Blue Card note
The employer's PPK contribution counts as your taxable income — but **under 26, PIT-0 makes
even that tax-free.** Another reason to lock in the efficient structure while young.

## 6. PPK (Employee Capital Plans) — stay in

### 6.1 Why it's worth it even if you might leave Poland
- **Your PPK is your private property** — leaving Poland does **not** forfeit it. You can
  leave it invested (take at 60, even from abroad) or withdraw early.
- **Employer 1.5% is free money.** Even on early withdrawal you keep **70%** of it.
- **Worst-case early cash-out still beats opting out:** official PPK data shows ~**+50%**
  vs contributions after ~1 year, ~**2×** after 10 years — *after* all penalties.

### 6.2 Contributions (2026)
| Source | Rate |
|---|---|
| You | 2% of gross (reducible to 0.5% only if earning ≤1.2× min wage — N/A for you) |
| Employer | 1.5% of gross |
| State | 250 PLN welcome (once) + 240 PLN/year |

### 6.3 Early-withdrawal (zwrot) penalties before age 60
- Lose **all state bonuses**.
- **30%** of employer contributions → moved to your ZUS account (locked until pension age —
  the weakest part for a leaver; but only ~0.45% of gross).
- **19%** tax on investment gains.

### 6.4 Angles specific to you
- **Under-26:** the employer contribution (normally taxable to you) is **tax-free** under PIT-0.
- **First home:** under 45 you may withdraw **up to 100% penalty-free toward a first-home
  down payment** (self-loan, repay over ≤15 yrs). PPK can become **part of your Warsaw
  down payment**, not locked-away money.
- **Auto re-enrollment every 4 years** (next **2027**) — if you opt out, you must re-opt-out each cycle.

### 6.5 Verdict
**Stay in.** Opt out only if you'd rather have ~2% of gross as cash *this month* for maximum
down-payment/B2B liquidity. For your income level the match outweighs that.

## 7. Warsaw apartment strategy (recap)
- **UoP secures the mortgage** (banks weight stable employment income); **B2B builds the
  down payment** faster.
- Aim for **20% down** to avoid low-deposit premiums and get better rates.
- **PPK first-home withdrawal** can supplement the down payment penalty-free (§6.4).
- Under-26 net is unusually high now → **front-load savings before the tax break ends.**
- After purchase, mortgage **overpayment (nadpłata)** shortens the loan dramatically when
  you direct a high share of net income at principal.

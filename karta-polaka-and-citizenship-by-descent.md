# Karta Polaka & Polish Citizenship by Descent — Wołyń Roots

> Deep-dive companion to `residency-tax-financial-plan.md`.
> For someone whose **grandmother was born in Wołyń (Volhynia)** — part of the
> Second Polish Republic (1921–1939). Compiled July 2026.
>
> ⚠️ Researched fact, not legal advice. The load-bearing questions (which route
> applies, whether the citizenship chain is unbroken) need a specialist's
> assessment of your actual family facts. Flagged items: ⚖️.

---

## 0. The key insight: there are TWO routes, and one is much better

A grandmother born in interwar Wołyń was almost certainly a **Polish citizen** under
the 1920 Citizenship Act. That opens a route most people miss:

1. **Citizenship by descent (confirmation / *potwierdzenie obywatelstwa*)** — you may
   **already be a Polish (EU) citizen**. Recognition, not a grant. **No residence, no
   language requirement, no waiting** — a passport directly, *if the chain holds.*
2. **Karta Polaka** — the fallback. Card → free permanent residence → citizenship after
   1 year. Cleaner to qualify for, but requires living in Poland + Polish language.

**Test route #1 first; #2 is the safety net.** The documents you gather are the same for both.

| | **Citizenship by descent** | **Karta Polaka** |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome | Polish/EU passport directly | Card → pobyt stały → citizenship ~1 yr |
| Residence in Poland | ❌ None | ✅ 1 yr on permanent residence |
| Language | ❌ None | ✅ Basic Polish now + **B1** for citizenship |
| Basis | Grandmother was a **citizen**, unbroken transmission | Grandmother was of Polish **nationality** |
| Certainty (your case) | ⚖️ Needs assessment (female-line risk) | ✅ One Polish grandparent = enough |
| Cost | PLN 277 gov fee + ~€2,000–5,000 docs/legal | Free card; ~100 PLN residence card |
| Speed | Assessment + docs, then months–~2 yrs for decision | ~2 mo card + 1 yr residence + citizenship |

---

## 1. ⚖️ The caveat that decides which route: the pre-1951 female line

Polish citizenship descent rules changed in 1951:
- **Before 19 Jan 1951:** a child born in wedlock took the **father's** citizenship. A Polish
  woman who **married a foreigner lost her Polish citizenship** on marriage.
- **From 1951:** both parents transmit equally.

**Implication for a grandmother line:** if your grandmother married a **non-Polish
(Ukrainian) man before 1951**, the citizenship chain *through her* may be **broken** — her
children would have taken the father's (non-Polish) citizenship. That pushes you to **Karta
Polaka** (which only needs her Polish *nationality*, not an unbroken *citizenship* chain).

**Facts that determine the outcome — get these:**
- Is the Polish ancestor your **grandmother or grandfather**? (Male line transmits more cleanly pre-1951.)
- Her/his **marriage date**, and whether the **spouse was also Polish**.
- Whether the line down to you **crosses 1951** (after which the female line works too).
- Any events that could have caused **loss** of citizenship (emigration/naturalization abroad,
  foreign military service).

Because a female-line, pre-war chain is exactly the tricky case, **let a specialist assess
feasibility before you spend on legal filing** (most offer this free — see §5).

## 2. Karta Polaka — eligibility (Ustawa o Karcie Polaka, art. 2)

Granted to a person who **declares belonging to the Polish Nation** and meets ALL of:
- **Basic knowledge of Polish** (regarded as mother tongue) + knowledge/cultivation of
  Polish traditions and customs.
- **Written declaration** of belonging to the Polish Nation before a consul / wojewoda.
- **Polish ancestry:** *at least one parent OR grandparent, OR two great-grandparents, was of
  Polish nationality or held Polish citizenship.*
  → **Your grandmother alone is sufficient.** You do not need great-grandparents.
- Declaration that neither you nor your ancestors were **repatriated from Poland to the USSR**
  under the 1944–1957 agreements.

**Alternative route (no ancestry):** a certificate from an authorized Polish organization
confirming ≥3 years active involvement in Polish language/culture. ⚠️ This is the route the
proposed reform wants to abolish — **irrelevant to you** since you rely on real ancestry.

**Cannot be granted if** you already hold Polish citizenship or a Polish permanent-residence
permit (unless stateless).

## 3. Documents that prove Polish roots (same for both routes)

You bear the burden of proof and must present **originals in person**. Accepted:
- **Civil-status records** (birth/*akt urodzenia*, marriage/*akt małżeństwa*) of you AND the
  Polish ancestor, establishing the kinship chain.
- **Baptism / church metrical records** (*metryki*) — **Roman Catholic parish records are
  treated as strong proof of Polish nationality.**
- **Pre-war Polish citizenship/ID documents** (Second Republic passport, dowód osobisty).
- School certificates, military records, documents recording *narodowość: polska*.

**Nationality vs. residence:** living in Wołyń is **not** by itself proof — it was
multi-ethnic (Poles, Ukrainians, Jews). You need a document recording the ancestor's **Polish
nationality or citizenship** (a Roman Catholic baptism record usually serves as the proxy).

**Strongest package for you:** grandmother's **birth/baptism record** (showing Polish
nationality/citizenship) + the **chain** grandmother → your parent → you (birth certificates),
+ her **marriage record** (settles the pre-1951 question).

## 4. Where the Wołyń records are

Once you know the **exact village/parish**, the record sits in one of these:

**Polish side**
- **AGAD** (Archiwum Główne Akt Dawnych, Warsaw) — holds many pre-war Wołyń church-book
  duplicates (Roman/Greek Catholic, Orthodox, Jewish, Lutheran). Portal: `szukajwarchiwach.gov.pl`.
- **USC** (Urząd Stanu Cywilnego) of the town where the family **settled in post-1945 Poland** —
  for later marriages/records; a certified *odpis zupełny* is what the citizenship file needs.

**Ukrainian oblast archives** (old Wołyń split across three):
- **DAWO** — Lutsk (Łuck): western/northern Wołyń (Łuck, Kowel, Włodzimierz districts) + interwar
  voivodeship admin records. `volyn.archives.gov.ua`
- **DARO** — Rivne (Równe): eastern Wołyń (Dubno, Zdołbunów, Kostopol, Równe, Sarny) — metric
  books c. 1840–1924. `rv.archives.gov.ua`
- **DAŻO** — Zhytomyr (Żytomierz): seat of the Łuck-Żytomierz Roman Catholic diocese — large runs
  of RC & Orthodox metrical books for Volhynia. `archive.zt.ua`

> You can't know which archive until you know the parish. Zhytomyr often holds the older
> Catholic diocesan duplicates; Lutsk/Rivne hold local originals by district.

## 5. How to do it — steps, tools, prices

### Step 1 — Gather from family (free, do first)
Full maiden name (+ Polish spellings), approx. birth year, **exact village/parish**, **religion**,
parents' names, **marriage date & place**, where she settled after the war. The **village** is
the single most valuable fact.

### Step 2 — Free online databases (a weekend, €0)
| Resource | URL | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Metryki Wołyń** (best for you) | wolyn-metryki.pl | 600k+ Roman Catholic Łuck/Lwów diocese records |
| **Geneteka** | geneteka.genealodzy.pl | Largest Polish index, 30M+ entries, free |
| **Szukaj w Archiwach** | szukajwarchiwach.gov.pl | Polish State Archives scans (incl. AGAD Wołyń) |
| **FamilySearch** | familysearch.org | Microfilmed church/civil records, free account |
| **DARO/DAŻO online** | rv.archives.gov.ua, archive.zt.ua | Ukrainian archive scans being posted |

### Step 3 — Order certified copies
- **DIY:** write to AGAD or the Ukrainian oblast archive (cheap, slow, Polish/Ukrainian
  correspondence, wartime delays).
- **Paid single-record retrieval:** ~**$200–300 per certified record**; +$200 for an in-Poland
  representative (e.g., Stories from Poland).

### Step 4 — Professional research firms & pricing
| Firm | Price | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **GenealogyTour** | $399–899 fixed packages | Best-value record search + translations |
| **PolishOrigins** | ~$900 entry (Kresy specialists) | Full Wołyń archive research |
| **Your Roots in Poland** | Free assessment, then custom | Named Karta Polaka + citizenship services |
| **Stories from Poland** | $200–300/record; $100–1,000+ projects | À-la-carte document retrieval |
| **Legacy Tree** | $3,200 (25h) – $11,500 (100h) | Premium full-service |
| **Citizenship law firms** — Lexmotion, CGO Legal, Polaron, findingpoland | €1,500–5,000 all-in | Legal filing for the passport |

*General East-European genealogist rates: $50–200/hr. Most firms give a **free feasibility
assessment** — treat quoted figures as ranges to confirm.*

### Step 5 — File the legal application
- **Citizenship confirmation:** gov fee **PLN 277 (~€58)**; + sworn translations (~$25–75/page,
  files run 20–50 pages) + apostilles (~$50–100/doc) + legal fees. **All-in ~€2,000–5,000;**
  simpler no-Ukraine-search cases ~£800–1,500. Voivode decision: months to ~1–2 years.
- **Karta Polaka:** free; basic-Polish interview + declaration at the **Wojewoda** (in Poland)
  or consulate. Processing ~2 months. Then free pobyt stały (~100 PLN card), then citizenship
  after 1 year (with a **B1 Polish** certificate).

### Realistic totals
- **DIY-lucky** (record online, order copies, self-file): **~$600–1,500**, a few months.
- **Full professional** (PL+UA search, translations, legal filing): **~€2,500–6,500**, ~6 mo–2 yrs.

## 6. Karta Polaka — what it gives (if that's your route)

- **Path:** valid Karta Polaka → **free permanent residence** (art. 195 ust. 1 pkt 9 ustawy o
  cudzoziemcach; fee waived, ~100 PLN card) → **citizenship by recognition** after **1 year**
  continuous residence on it (art. 30 ust. 1 pkt 4/5 ustawy o obywatelstwie; **fee-exempt**),
  with a **B1 Polish** certificate.
- **Other benefits:** work without a permit; run a business as a Pole; free education +
  scholarships; emergency healthcare; 37% rail discount; free state museums.
- You **surrender the Karta Polaka** once you obtain permanent residence.

## 7. Risk flags ⚖️
- **Oct 2024 migration strategy** would (a) abolish the no-ancestry "3-year activity" route and
  (b) add/strengthen a language requirement on citizenship. **Doesn't hurt a genuine grandparent
  claim**, but makes solid ancestry documents essential. Re-verify the statute (Dz.U.2026.76)
  before filing.
- **Settlement cash benefit (świadczenie pieniężne) suspended** since 22 Jan 2025 — don't budget for it.
- **Polish consular services in Ukraine are war-suspended** — use the **Wojewoda** route in Poland.
- **Language appears twice** for the Karta Polaka route: basic spoken for the card, **B1
  certificate** for citizenship. Start Polish study early (easy for a Ukrainian speaker).

## 9. Why this changes your whole plan

If either route works, it **collapses the 8-year Poland timeline** from
`residency-tax-financial-plan.md`:
- **Citizenship by descent** → EU passport with **no residence/language/wait** at all.
- **Karta Polaka** → citizenship in **~1 year** of permanent residence (vs. ~8 years the standard way).

Either way it also makes the CUKR-vs-Blue-Card and TP-counting questions **much less important** —
you'd be on a far faster track. **This is the highest-leverage thing to investigate**, and the
first step (talking to family + free database searches) costs nothing.
